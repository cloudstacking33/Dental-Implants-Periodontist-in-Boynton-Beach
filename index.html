Frequently asked questions

What is a dental implants periodontist?

A periodontist is a dental professional who focuses on the gums and the bone that support your teeth. A dental implants periodontist applies that knowledge to implant placement, so your new teeth rest on healthy, stable tissue.

Are dental implants permanent?

The titanium implant is designed to bond with your jawbone and can last many years with good oral hygiene and regular checkups. The visible crown or denture on top may need maintenance or replacement over time.

Am I a candidate for same day dental implants?

Many patients are, but it depends on bone quality, gum health, and your overall health. We confirm eligibility during your implant consultation after reviewing your imaging.

What if I do not have enough bone for implants?

Limited bone does not always rule out implants. Depending on your situation, options may include bone grafting, angled implant placement, or zygomatic dental implants anchored in the cheekbone.

Can a failed dental implant be fixed?

In many cases, yes. We evaluate why the implant is struggling, treat infection or bone loss when possible, and discuss dental implant rescue, revision, or replacement.

What payment methods do you accept?

We accept cash and credit cards. Please call our office if you would like to discuss payment before your visit.

Dental Implants

Dental implants are small titanium posts placed in the jawbone to act as artificial tooth roots. Once the implant bonds with the bone, a crown, bridge, or denture is attached on top. All on 4 Dental Implants

All on 4 dental implants use four carefully positioned implants to support a complete arch of replacement teeth. Dental Implant Rescue

Dental implant rescue is aimed at saving an implant that is in trouble before it is lost. When caught early, many implant problems can be stabilized. The most common issue is peri implant disease, an infection of the gum and bone around the implant that behaves much like gum disease around natural teeth. Treatment may include deep cleaning, targeted therapy to remove bacteria, bone regeneration, and guidance on home care. Because this is gum and bone care, a periodontist led approach is well suited to the task. We assess how much support remains, explain the likelihood of success, and discuss alternatives if saving the implant is not realistic. Early attention makes a real difference, so we encourage you to call at the first sign of tenderness, swelling, or bleeding. Our Boynton Beach team is ready to evaluate your implant and outline a clear plan.
